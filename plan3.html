How to Balance Social and School Activities

Specific Objective

Students will be able to listen to an interview about school-life balance and interpret key suggestions for relaxation in pairs during a graphic organizer completion activity.

Learning Outcomes

  • Can describe the storyline of an audio or visual narrative about balancing school and personal life.
  • Can comprehend extended discussions on strategies for learning to relax and maintaining well-being.

🪢 Key Concept: Boundaries

A boundary is a limit that separates different areas of life — like keeping school work out of personal time, just like a rope divides two sides of a space.

Stage 2 (Reading)Editorial: Student Stress & Boundaries
16 Min

Read paragraphs 1 and 2 of the editorial. Find the answers to the 3 questions on the board. Click the 2 second conditional sentences.

Managing Stress: The Importance of Boundaries

Paragraph 1: High school students today face enormous pressure. Between academic deadlines and social expectations, many feel overwhelmed. Research shows that students who set clear boundaries between school and personal time tend to have lower stress levels. If students learned to say no to extra commitments, they would have more energy for essential tasks.

Paragraph 2: The solution lies in self-awareness. Students must recognize their personal limits. Creating a weekly schedule that includes downtime is a proven strategy. If teenagers managed their screen time better, they could improve their concentration significantly. Schools can support this by teaching time-management skills.

Second Conditional Structure (Click the sentences above to highlight them):
If + subject + past simple, subject + would/could + base verb
  • "If students learned to say no, they would have more energy."
  • "If teenagers managed screen time, they could improve concentration."

Stage 2 (Speaking)Model Dialogue – PPC
20 Min

Listen and read the model dialogue. Identify 2 examples of Present Perfect Continuous. Practice reading chorally in pairs.

Student A:Hey, you look tired. What have you been doing lately?
Student B:I know! I have been trying to balance football and my exams. It's really exhausting.
Student A:I understand. You might want to set aside one free afternoon each week. That could really help.
Student B:That sounds useful. I'll give it a try!
Phrases for Giving Suggestions:
  • "You might want to..."
  • "That could really help."
  • "Have you considered...?"
